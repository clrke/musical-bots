The bots of this street are the cast of Musical Bots, and this episode is the true story of how
Musical Bots was made for the Meta Global AI Hackathon. The bots sing about building themselves.

The event (dev.meta.ai/events/global-hackathon):
- The Meta Global AI Hackathon: ten days, online, builders from around the world. It opens with a
  kickoff livestream; Meta and its partners run workshops; projects are due Nov 4.
- The bots enter the Indie developer track (one builder plus the bots).
- The judges look for three things: creativity, technical ambition, and whether it works.
- Judging runs Nov 4-8; the winners are named at the closing ceremony, live finals on Nov 9.
- There is NO trophy, medal, cup or gold prize. The win is their name called at the closing show
  (a cash prize pool is shared by the winners: do not sing about money).

Their entry: Musical Bots. You type a one-line cartoon idea and press Premiere; the sixteen bots
write the song, stage it and sing it as a 3D musical in the browser, every voice synthesized live
(no recordings). Heads and floating hands, no bodies; the diner, the big house and the street are
the sets they built. The bots are named by colour. The crew of eight: Green (lyrics), White
(chords), Gray (dance), Blue (lighting), Gold (deals: keeps the time and the budget), Yellow (hype),
Pink (comedy), Black (plot trouble: he dreams up the problems, and joined the crew on Nov 1). The
rest of the cast: Silver, Purple, Orange, Jade, Navy, Copper, Lilac, Red.

How it was made (the team's own bugs, found and fixed together, in this order):
1. Oct 28, morning: the builder writes the code file by file with an AI coding assistant:
   a blank stage, then heads, floating hands and the three sets. Muse Code directs the crew.
2. Oct 28-29, the voice: the bots sing for the first time, but some words come out as mush: "box"
   blurs at the end of a line, "hackathon" comes out hack-KA-thun. The fix: the singer learns a
   dictionary of twenty thousand words and how each one is said.
3. Oct 29, the lyrics: a gate checks every line (does it rhyme? does it fit the beat?) and stamps
   "no!" on line after line. Green writes it again, and again, until the lines ring.
4. Oct 29, the hands: the high-five whiffs, the handshake grabs air, a hand covers the wrong eye in
   peekaboo. The fix: every hand play is choreographed, and now the palms really meet on the beat.
5. Oct 29, afternoon, the camera and the lights: the camera stares up at the clouds (it framed a
   singer still walking in), and the spotlight burns the walls white. Blue turns the lamp down, the
   camera waits for everyone to arrive.
6. Oct 29, the sound: the backing choir drowns the words, so the team plays each song to a listening
   machine, counts the words it catches, and turns the choir down. Pressing Premiere twice played two
   songs at once; now the newest one wins. A tap on a phone starts the sound too.
7. Oct 29, late at night, bumping heads: bots glide right through each other. The team makes a rule:
   look ahead, and when two meet head-on, each steps to its own right. Nobody bumps any more.
8. Oct 30: the crew sings its own making-of, finishes days before the Nov 4 deadline, and waits for
   the show.

Late nights with Muse (from the git log; the builder's clock, UTC+8):
- The repository has about 190 commits, from the first on Oct 28 at 10:03 in the morning to Nov 1
  after four in the morning. Nearly thirty of them were made between midnight and six in the
  morning, on three nights: the team codes with Muse Code long after bedtime.
- Night one, Oct 29, just after midnight (12:19 to 12:37 am): 7 commits in 18 minutes, the
  submission's first draft, the trailer's typed opening, and each singer's job shown on screen.
- Night two, Oct 31 (12:10 to 2:55 am): at 2:41 am the camera, which swung between three angles
  every half second, learns to follow the bots smoothly.
- Night three, Nov 1 (from 1:48 am to after 4 am), the longest: 16 commits by 3:49 am, and more. The making-of turns into a pure
  voices-only song, each bot answers a crewmate's question right away, and at 1:57 am a funny bug is
  fixed: the name "White", held for two beats, came out as "Why". Now the last sound rings and
  "White" is "White" again. At 3:49 am the new trailer is cut, and after four in the morning Black
  joins the crew as the plot's trouble (the bot who dreams up the problems in this very song).
- The picture: a laptop glowing in the dark, the builder yawning, Muse still typing ("just one more
  fix"), a bug found and fixed while the street sleeps. Muse is a tireless, cheerful teammate.

Story notes: the fun comes from the bugs (every mistake becomes part of the show) and from fixing
them together. Concrete, kid-friendly pictures: a laptop glowing at night, a gate stamping "no!", a
word sung as mush, a high-five that whiffs, a camera staring at the clouds, walls glowing white, two
bots bumping heads. The lead can be Green (the lyrics bot, who keeps getting lines stamped "no!").
The lyrics say "we" (the team builds it together), not "I".
