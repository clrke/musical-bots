A sunny-day seaside cartoon for small children. Nothing here is real-world news: no web facts are
needed. The fun is the chase, Silver's bad luck, and friends fixing it together.

The story (keep its places in this order, one place per stretch of beats):
- The pool (the start): a hot day at the outdoor pool. Silver, the unlucky one ("Just my luck"),
  brings a big striped beach ball. Jade dares a cannonball off the diving board; the splash knocks
  the ball over the fence and away.
- The beach (the chase): the ball rolls down the sand past the umbrella, bumps the sandcastle and
  the lifeguard tower; Silver and Jade run after it (Silver will not step on the cracks in the
  boardwalk, so he is always one step behind). Black laughs and bets they can't catch it.
- The pier (the low point and the rescue): the ball bounces along the docks at golden hour and
  drifts off the pier's end out to sea. The grown-up, Orange (Dad, the PE teacher), stops the
  panic: we never jump in after it; we work as a team. Black, the rival, has a soft spot: he
  lends his fishing net. Jade's courage and Silver's careful counting of the waves only work
  together: Silver counts, Jade reaches, the net scoops the ball home.
- The ending: back on the pier at sunset, the whole crowd plays catch, and Silver says his luck
  has changed. The irony: the ball Silver was sure was lost brings everyone together.

Sets: use pool, then beach, then pier (the pier holds the bridges, chorus 3 and the outro). The
ocean (the log raft) may appear only if the plan needs it; prefer pool, beach and pier.

Tone (must-haves):
- Kind and silly, never scary: the sea is calm and sunny, nobody is in danger, nobody swims out.
- The lyrics say "we" (the friends chase it together), never "I".
- The bots on stage talk to each other by name ("Jade, look!", "Silver, count the waves"). Nobody
  sings to the audience or about the show, the camera or the song: no fourth wall.
- Concrete pictures: a striped beach ball, a cannonball splash, a sandcastle bump, the pier's end,
  a fishing net, a game of catch at sunset.
